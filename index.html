01

A retention process built from zero

Retention · Measurement

42% → 70%win-back rate after refocusing the targeting, then scaled from 10 to 181 customers a month

Problem

Our highest-value customers left quietly. There was no process to spot them, contact them or check if it worked.

What I did

A three-step cycle. Find at-risk customers from spend and behavior signals. Reach out in a personal way. Measure with two rules only: did they pay again, and did their behavior improve. Opens and clicks don't count.

Result

Narrowing the targeting lifted the win-back rate from 42% to 70%. Scaled across two brands in one month: 181 contacted, 62 back. Next step: a holdout group to prove the lift is causal.

For you

The same loop works for self-serve accounts: signal, play, measure against a control group.

02

Automated risk alerts and a daily contact list

Signals · Automation · Experiments

4 signalschecked automatically every week, plus a daily dashboard of who to contact and why

Problem

Risk was found by reviewing data by hand, usually too late.

What I did

Built automated weekly alerts on product data for four signals: a drop in spending, a support conflict, a customer moving away from their usual provider, and a customer close to their spending limit. Added a daily dashboard of the top accounts with a ranked contact list. Then moved the outreach into a marketing automation platform and A/B tested the messages.

Result

The team starts each day with a list instead of a search. Outreach went from 10 to 181 accounts a month with the same team. Personalizing the message lifted replies from 6% to 11%. The spending-limit alert lets us reach customers before they hit the cap, right when they want to do more.

For you

"Close to a spending limit" is the same moment as "close to a contact limit": the right time to talk about a bigger plan.

03

Automated social media replies with ManyChat and Zendesk

Customer Support role · 2022–2023

2 brandswith Facebook and Instagram messages answered automatically

Problem

The brands' Facebook and Instagram inboxes were answered by hand, which was slow for customers and for the support team.

What I did

Built a ManyChat flow that answers incoming messages automatically, and connected it to Zendesk so the support team could see every conversation and step in when needed.

Result

Faster, consistent replies on social media, with less manual work for the team and full visibility in one place.

How I'd approach LATAM self-serve

Make the base visible, test, then scale what works

A first draft of how I'd start as the first Account Manager for a base of self-serve customers who have never talked to a person.

Never activated

Activation rescue

Paid, but WhatsApp isn't connected. A short setup call in their language.

At risk

Save before cancel

Usage dropping or a failed payment. A WhatsApp check-in, then find the real cause.

Ready to grow

Right-size the plan

Near contact, seat or AI limits. Show when the next plan costs less than overage.

Healthy

Light-touch nurture

Stable usage. Tips on features they don't use yet, and watch for triggers.

Days 1–30

Learn

Pull every account. Read 90 days of cancellations. Talk to ~25 customers in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia. Health score v1.

Days 31–60

Test

Two experiments with holdout groups: activation rescue and right-sizing. WhatsApp-first outreach in PT, ES and EN.

Days 61–90

Scale

Keep what moved revenue. Write playbook v1, automate the alerts, propose KPIs and the next hire.

How I'd measure it: expansion revenue, saved revenue and net revenue retention, always against a control group. Plus one behavior rule: did the account move to a healthier segment.
